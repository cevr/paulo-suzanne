# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is hungry local customers and visitors deciding what to eat, whether Paulo & Suzanne is open, and how to order or get there. They may arrive on a phone, late at night, and need useful answers immediately in French or English.

The supporting audience is the restaurant team. Nontechnical editors need to keep the bilingual website accurate without understanding storage, deployments, schemas, or code.

## Product Purpose

The public website turns Paulo & Suzanne’s reputation into an immediate visit or order. It presents the menu, story, location, hours, and contact information with the same direct energy as the restaurant itself.

Success means visitors can recognize the restaurant, find the information they need, and confidently view the menu, order online, call, or visit. It also means the restaurant team can keep that information current through a safe, understandable editorial workflow.

## Positioning

Paulo & Suzanne is Montréal’s original bilingual casse-croûte: legendary poutine, burgers, and generous hospitality rooted in the same neighborhood since 1980.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: view the menu. Transactional follow-through: order online.
- Secondary paths: find the restaurant, check opening hours, call, or get in touch.
- The line visitors should remember after ten seconds: “The Original Casse-Croûte — legendary poutine and burgers since 1980.”
- Belief ladder:
  1. This is the recognizable Paulo & Suzanne restaurant visitors are looking for.
  2. It is an established Montréal institution with more than four decades of history.
  3. The food is generous, familiar, and worth the trip or order.
  4. The restaurant is easy to find, open late, and ready to serve them.
- Proof on hand: operating since March 18, 1980; a three-generation family story; a published satisfaction promise; real menu photography; current address, hours, phone number, and social profiles in `app/content/defaults.ts`; restaurant photography and logo assets in `public/`.

## Brand Personality

Energetic, nostalgic, welcoming.

The voice is bilingual, direct, playful, and proudly local. It should feel like a beloved neighborhood counter with personality—not a corporate campaign. Confidence comes from specific food, history, people, and place rather than inflated claims.

## Anti-references

Do not make Paulo & Suzanne look like a restrained fine-dining restaurant, a generic fast-food franchise, or a polished technology startup. Avoid anonymous stock-food aesthetics, luxury minimalism, delicate editorial styling, and technical CMS language that puts infrastructure between the restaurant team and their content.

## Design Principles

1. **Lead with appetite and recognition.** Food, logo, name, and the primary action should answer “Is this the place?” immediately.
2. **Make practical information effortless.** Menu, ordering, hours, location, phone, and language switching should never require exploration.
3. **Earn nostalgia with real history.** Use the restaurant’s people, dates, photography, and neighborhood story instead of generic retro decoration.
4. **Keep both languages first-class.** French and English content must carry equal meaning, hierarchy, and usability.
5. **Let the team edit without technical translation.** Editorial tools should use restaurant language, preserve work safely, and clearly distinguish private drafts from the live website.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The website must remain usable by keyboard and touch, communicate state without color alone, provide meaningful bilingual alternative text and control labels, preserve visible focus, and respect reduced-motion preferences. Controls should remain comfortably sized on mobile, and essential restaurant information must stay readable at common zoom levels and viewport sizes.
